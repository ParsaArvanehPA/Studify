🎯 "Problem" = 3 Different Terms

The word "problem" looks like one concept but is THREE, each belonging to a different discourse context.

📌 The Three Problems

Problem1: SOURCE-oriented, PROSPECTIVE. About translatability. Utopian, abstract, NEVER actually solved. Theoretical persona of translator. "Game with complete information."
Problem2: TARGET-oriented, RETROSPECTIVE. Reconstructed from coupled pairs in actual translations. Concrete, realized. Solution2 = whatever is in the text.
Problem3: PROCESS-oriented, PROCESSUAL. Reconstructed step by step. Allows INTERIM SOLUTIONS discovered via think-aloud, keystroke logging (Translog). Dynamic — the problem can change during the act.

📌 Pseudotranslations (Excursus A)

Definition: Text composed as-if-translated but with NO real source. Activates Source-Text Postulate.
Why significant: Reveal how a culture views translation. Safe way to introduce novelties (deviations tolerated more in "translations"). Individual reinvention. Importing prestige. Culture planning.
Key example — Papa Hamlet (1889): Holz & Schlaf published their own German stories as "translated from Norwegian." Accepted by critics as genuine. Helped launch German Naturalism. Proves pseudotranslations can be effective literary vehicles.
