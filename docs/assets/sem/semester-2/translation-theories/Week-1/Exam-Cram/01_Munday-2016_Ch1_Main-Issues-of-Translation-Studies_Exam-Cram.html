⚡ Main Issues of Translation Studies · EXAM CRAM

Jeremy Munday, Ch. 1 | Just the essentials

🎯 Big Picture

Translation is ancient; translation studies as a DISCIPLINE is young (born ~1972). The chapter gives you the definitions, the history of how the field became a field, and the maps scholars use to organize it.

📌 Translation = 3 Different Things

Field ("I studied at university")
Product (the actual translated text)
Process (the act of translating)

📌 Jakobson's Three Categories — EXAM GOLD

Intralingual — same language, rewording (summaries, children's versions)
Interlingual — between languages ("translation proper")
Intersemiotic — verbal signs → non-verbal (novel → film)

📌 Holmes/Toury Map — DRAW THIS

PURE → Theoretical: general (all translation) vs partial (restricted by medium/area/rank/text-type/time/problem)
PURE → Descriptive (DTS): product-oriented (existing translations), function-oriented (role in target culture), process-oriented (what's in the translator's mind)
APPLIED: translator training, translation aids, translation criticism, translation policy
💡 KEY: DTS feeds back into theory. The branches INTERACT.

📌 van Doorslaer's Addition

Strategy = overall orientation of a text (e.g. literal translation)
Procedure = specific technique at one point in the text (e.g. borrowing, calque)

📌 Why It Took So Long

  • Grammar-translation method (18th c.-1960s) made translation look like a mechanical exercise
  • Communicative approach (1960s-70s) abandoned translation in language teaching
  • Holmes (1972) finally gave it a name and a map
