⚡ The Tradition of Translation in the Western World · EXAM CRAM

Eugene Nida, Ch. 2 | Just the essentials

🎯 Big Picture

A history from the Rosetta Stone to machine translation. Two conflicts NEVER go away: (1) literal vs. free (a scale, not a binary) and (2) form vs. content. Bible translation adds special theological problems.

📌 The Timeline (Know These)

Rosetta Stone (2nd c. BCE) — unlocked Egyptian hieroglyphics
Septuagint (3rd-2nd c. BCE) — Greek OT; first major Western translation
St Jerome (395 CE) — Vulgate; sense-for-sense except Scripture
Luther (1522-34) — German Bible; concrete translation principles
Dolet (1540) — FIRST theory of translation; five ranked principles; executed
Dryden (1680) — metaphrase/paraphrase/imitation
Tytler (1790) — first systematic English study; three laws
Matthew Arnold / Revised Version — 19th c. pedantic literalism

📌 Dolet's Five Principles (First Theory!)

  • Perfectly understand the author's content and intention
  • Perfect knowledge of both SL and TL
  • Avoid word-for-word — destroys meaning AND beauty
  • Use common speech
  • Right overall "tone" through word choice and order

📌 The Two Conflicts

Literal ↔ Free (a scale, demonstrated by ASV→RSV→NEB→Phillips Bible versions)
Form ↔ Content (especially acute in poetry — "Poesie into Poesie")

📌 Three Theological Problems (Bible Translation)

Inspiration vs. Philology: Augustine (Septuagint was divinely inspired) vs. Jerome (philological scholarship; modern scholarship sides with Jerome)
Tradition vs. Authority: what the Church has always used vs. what scholarship says
Theology vs. Grammar: when the grammar forces a theological interpretation

📌 Five 20th-century Developments

  • Structural linguistics (Saussure, Prague School)
  • Wycliffe Bible Translators / SIL (applied linguistics)
  • United Bible Societies (The Bible Translator journal)
  • Babel journal (UNESCO / FIT)
  • Machine translation (even imperfect, advanced semantic theory)
