⚡ Introduction: Toward a Science of Translating · EXAM CRAM

Eugene Nida, Ch. 1 | Just the essentials

🎯 Big Picture

Practice has "far outdistanced" theory. Nida wants translation studied as a descriptive science — like linguistics. He draws on EVERYTHING: anthropology, psychology, philosophy, Chomskyan grammar.

📌 Key Claims

"Science of translating" = the descriptive study of the transfer process. NOT saying it's purely mechanical — art is still essential. But the PROCESSES can be described.
Two tensions: form vs. meaning (preserving style = losing content, and vice versa); letter vs. spirit (the substance vs. the "feeling")

📌 Jakobson's Three Types (as used by Nida)

Intralingual — rewording within the same language
Interlingual — between languages ("translation proper")
Intersemiotic — between sign systems (verbal → film, etc.)

📌 Three Kinds of Restriction

  • Cultural: Jiggs's corned beef → different foods in different cultures
  • Poetic/form: In poetry, form IS meaning — can't separate them
  • Media: Dubbing must match timing, syllables, facial movements

📌 Why Bible Translation?

💡 Richest dataset: 1,200+ languages, 2,000+ years, every type of text (poetry, law, proverbs, narrative). Not that the book is ONLY about Bible translation — but it provides the most data.
