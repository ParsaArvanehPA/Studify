⚡ Equivalence and Equivalent Effect · EXAM CRAM

Jeremy Munday, Ch. 3 | Just the essentials

🎯 Big Picture

To escape the vague "literal vs. free" debate, 1950s-60s theorists got systematic about meaning and equivalence. The chapter runs: Jakobson → Nida (formal/dynamic + equivalent effect) → Newmark (semantic/communicative) → Koller (5 types) → case studies proving it all matters.

📌 Jakobson's Key Claim

"No full equivalence between code-units" (e.g. Russian syr ≠ English cheese). BUT "all is conveyable in any existing language." The exception is POETRY (unity of form/sense) — needs "creative transposition."

📌 Nida: Formal vs. Dynamic Equivalence

FORMAL: ST-oriented, message in form+content, gloss translations with footnotes. DYNAMIC: receptor-oriented, "closest natural equivalent," equivalent effect.
Priority rule: meaning over style
4 requirements: make sense / convey spirit / natural form / similar response
3-stage process: analysis → transfer → restructuring (Chomsky's kernels)

📌 The Genesis Case Study — EXAM GOLD

KJV = formal ("face of the deep," "Spirit of God," threefold "and") ↔ NEB = dynamic ("surface of the watery deep") ↔ NAB = very dynamic ("mighty wind" for "Spirit of God"). Shows the spectrum even within dynamic equivalence.

📌 The Maastricht Treaty Example

EU law = all versions equally valid → high formal equivalence. BUT English = "creating an ever closer union" (ongoing) vs. French = goal achieved. Shows limits.

📌 Newmark: Semantic vs. Communicative

SemanticCommunicative
SL culture, overtranslatesTL culture, undertranslates
Complex, detailedSmooth, clear, direct
Serious literatureMost texts (technical, informative)

📌 Koller's 5 Equivalence Types

Denotative (content - try first) → Connotative (near-synonyms, hardest) → Text-normative (text type norms) → Pragmatic (receiver = Nida's dynamic) → Formal (aesthetics, wordplay)

📌 Pym: Natural vs. Directional

Natural = identifying existing equivalents (glossaries, term bases). Directional = rendering ST meaning (CAT tools, TMs impose directional equivalents).
