⚡ Translation Theory Before the 20th Century · EXAM CRAM

Jeremy Munday, Ch. 2 | Just the essentials

🎯 The Core

2,000 years of Western theory = ONE debate: literal (word-for-word) vs. free (sense-for-sense).

📌 The Chain You MUST Know

Cicero (46 BCE): translate as an orator, not an interpreter
St Jerome (395 CE): sense-for-sense, EXCEPT Scripture (syntax = mystery)
Luther (1522): vernacular Bible; language of the common people
Dolet (1540): five ranked principles — first systematic theory
Dryden (1680): metaphrase / paraphrase (preferred) / imitation
Tytler (1790): three laws — first systematic English study
Schleiermacher (1813): foreignizing (move reader to writer) vs. domesticating
Yan Fu (1901): xin (fidelity), da (fluency), ya (elegance)

📌 Non-Western Also Matters

Chinese sutra translation: Three phases (literal → free → balanced). Dao'an's 5 losses + 3 difficulties.
Arabic Abbasid (Baghdad): Two methods — literal (Ibn al-Batriq) vs. sense-for-sense (Ibn Ishaq). Gutas: different "translation complexes."

📌 The Proust Case Study — Great for Essays

💡 Kilmartin (1981): criticized literal translation — "whiff of Gallicism." Wanted naturalizing. ↔ Prendergast (2002): argued "oddly unEnglish shapes" preserve Proust's estranging force. Foreignizing. The 2,000-year-old debate happening NOW.

📌 Key Terms

TermWhat It Means

🔥 BOTTOM LINE: Everything orbits the literal/free debate. Cicero → Jerome → Luther → Dryden → Dolet/Tytler → Schleiermacher. The Proust case shows it's still alive.
