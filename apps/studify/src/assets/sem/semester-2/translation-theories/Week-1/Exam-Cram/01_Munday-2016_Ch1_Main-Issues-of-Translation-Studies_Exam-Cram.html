⚡ Main Issues of Translation Studies · EXAM CRAM

Jeremy Munday, Introducing Translation Studies (2016), Ch. 1 | Essential Points Only

🎯 Core Argument

Central thesis: The practice of translation is ancient, but translation studies as an academic discipline is young — emerging only in the second half of the 20th century. The chapter defines what translation is, sketches the field's early history, and presents the maps (Holmes/Toury and van Doorslaer) that organise it.

📚 Key Terms & Concepts

Translation (3 senses): (1) the general subject field/phenomenon; (2) the product (the translated text); (3) the process of producing it (translating)
ST / SL: Source text / source language — what you translate from
TT / TL: Target text / target language — what you translate into
Interpreting: Oral translation; Otto Kade: the SL text is presented only once and cannot be reviewed, and the TL text is produced under time pressure with little chance for correction
Is interdisciplinarity a strength or a threat? Daniel Gile warns that partnerships are often unbalanced — status and funding lie with the partner discipline — which may weaken translation studies' autonomy.

🗝️ Rapid Q&A

Q: Distinguish the three meanings of the word 'translation'.
A: (1) the general subject field/phenomenon ('I studied translation'); (2) the product, i.e. the translated text; (3) the process of producing it, i.e. translating.
Q: Name and define Jakobson's three categories of translation.
A: Intralingual (rewording within the same language), interlingual ('translation proper', between two languages), and intersemiotic ('transmutation', between verbal and non-verbal sign systems, e.g. novel → film).
Q: Why is James Holmes important, and what did his 1972 paper do?
A: He gave the discipline its name ('translation studies') and, in 'The name and nature of translation studies', proposed an overall framework/map of the field — widely regarded as its founding statement.
Q: Explain the three kinds of descriptive translation studies (DTS).
A: Product-oriented (analysis of existing translations), function-oriented (their role in the recipient socioculture — contexts rather than texts), and process-oriented (the psychology of translating; think-aloud protocols, eye-tracking).
Q: What is the difference between a translation 'strategy' and a 'procedure' (van Doorslaer)?
A: A strategy is the overall orientation of a translated text (e.g. literal translation); a procedure is a specific technique used at a given point in the text (e.g. borrowing, calque).
🔥 Old practice, young discipline. Jakobson's three types + the Holmes/Toury map (pure = theoretical + descriptive; applied) are the exam backbone. Translation studies is now an interdiscipline.
