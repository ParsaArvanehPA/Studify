⚡ Translations as Facts of a 'Target' Culture · EXAM CRAM

Gideon Toury, Descriptive Translation Studies – and beyond, Ch. 1 | Essential Points Only

🎯 Core Argument

Central thesis: Translations are facts of the culture that hosts them. Their position, function and form are determined first and foremost by considerations originating in the target culture, not the source. This target-oriented axiom underpins Descriptive Translation Studies (DTS) and leads to the key working concept of the 'assumed translation'.

📚 Key Terms & Concepts

Source-Text Postulate: The assumption that there exists another text, in another culture/language, with chronological and logical priority, which served as a point of departure — even if it cannot actually be located (hence pseudotranslations are legitimate objects of study)
Transfer Postulate: The assumption that the process involved a transfer from the assumed source text of certain features the two now share
Relationship Postulate: The assumption that there are tangible relationships tying the translation to its assumed original, based on the shared/transferred features

🗝️ Rapid Q&A

Q: What does Toury mean by 'translations are facts of the target culture'?
A: That a translation's position, function and systemic status are determined first and foremost by considerations originating in the host (target) culture — not the source — and are constituted within it. This is the target-oriented axiom of DTS.
Q: How does Toury's programme differ from Skopostheorie, though both are target-oriented?
A: Skopos theory is applied — it seeks to make improvements/changes in practice; Toury's is descriptive-explanatory — it seeks exhaustive accounts of what is regarded as translational, aiming at generalizations about translational behaviour.
Q: Why does Toury prefer 'assumed translation' to a fixed definition?
A: Because restrictive, essentialist definitions shrink the field and cause circular reasoning. 'Assumed translation' — anything presented or regarded as a translation — expands the range of objects of study and stays operable even when no source text can be found (e.g. pseudotranslations).
Q: State the three postulates of 'assumed translation'.
A: The Source-Text Postulate (a prior text existed elsewhere), the Transfer Postulate (features were transferred from it), and the Relationship Postulate (tangible relationships tie the translation to its assumed original).
Q: How does Toury redefine equivalence?
A: Not prescriptively: he takes it as given that a TT is 'equivalent' to its ST and instead identifies the web of relations between them. Equivalence is the translation relationship that constitutes the norm for the pair of texts studied.
🔥 Translations are target-culture facts. Toury's descriptive programme replaces fixed definitions with 'assumed translation' (Source-Text + Transfer + Relationship postulates) and treats equivalence as a given norm-relationship to be described, not prescribed.
