⚡ What Is a Translation Theory? · EXAM CRAM

Anthony Pym, Ch. 1 | Just the essentials

🎯 Big Picture

All translators theorize whether they like it or not. Choosing between translation options = theorizing. Pym organizes the whole field by paradigms (not theorists).

📌 The Chain

Theorizing → internal, private, everyone does it
Theories → become public when there's DISAGREEMENT about how to translate
Paradigms → sets of principles shared by groups of theories (Kuhn)

📌 The "Tory" Example

💡 One term, many possible translations: keep+footnote / give equivalent / omit. Choosing = theorizing. Each option implies a DIFFERENT THEORY of what translation should do.

📌 Pym's Six Paradigms

1. Equivalence (natural + directional sub-types)
2. Purposes (Skopos — purpose determines method)
3. Descriptions (DTS — what translators actually do)
4. Uncertainty (deconstruction — present throughout history)
5. Localization (tech-driven, markets)
6. Cultural translation (broader cultural/political)
Key: Newer ≠ replacement. Equivalence lives on in CAT tools. Skopos can embed equivalence as a special case. Uncertainty is the real clash — it rejects both.

📌 Paradigm Clash Outcomes

Tension (endless debate) | Revolution (one wins) | Mutual ignorance (most common — Pym wants to fix this)

📌 Why Study Theory?

No empirical proof it makes you a better translator (untrained people may even be faster). But it:
  • Helps with problems that have NO established solution
  • Acts as an agent of change (e.g. "saying later" vs. "carrying across")
  • Raises the public image of translators
  • Gives you tools to DEFEND positions and UNDERSTAND others
  • Widens your range of generative options + gives you reasons to SELECT
