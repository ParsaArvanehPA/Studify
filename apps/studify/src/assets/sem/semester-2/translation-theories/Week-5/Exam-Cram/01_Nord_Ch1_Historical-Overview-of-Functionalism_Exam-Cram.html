⚡ Functionalism: A Historical Overview · EXAM CRAM

Christiane Nord, Ch. 1 | Just the essentials

🎯 Big Picture

Functionalism grew from dissatisfaction with equivalence among TRANSLATOR TRAINING scholars. Professionals often translate cases where equivalence is NOT wanted (e.g. a UK school certificate → German uni). The "German School" said: look at the PURPOSE, not just the source text.

📌 The Shift

From: equivalence (ST is the "measure of all things") → TO: function (purpose determines method)

📌 The Four Key Figures

Reiss (1971): functional criticism still within equivalence; exceptions when brief specifies different function or audience
Vermeer (1978): SKOPOS = purpose is the prime rule. ST = an "offer of information." The addressee is the key factor.
Holz-Mänttäri (1981/84): "translational action." Avoids the word "translation." Translators = experts producing "message transmitters."
Hönig & Kussmaul (1982): "necessary degree of precision" — reproduce only the relevant semantic features

📌 Nida's Role as Precursor

Dynamic equivalence already emphasized communicative effect. His 1976 framework asked "Best for whom?" — purpose matters. But his LINGUISTIC side (Chomsky's kernels) got more attention.

📌 Koller's Equivalence Problem

Koller: any TT not equivalent = non-translation. BUT he allowed "ad hoc adaptation" for pragmatic equivalence. The criteria were arbitrary — different standards for different text types.

📌 Common Functionalist Focus

  • Language-independent pragmatic/cultural aspects
  • Translation competence over language proficiency
  • What professionals actually DO, not abstract equivalence
