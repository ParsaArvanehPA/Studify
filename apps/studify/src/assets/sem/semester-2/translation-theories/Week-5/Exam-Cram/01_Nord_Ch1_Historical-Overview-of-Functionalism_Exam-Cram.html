⚡ Functionalism: A Historical Overview · EXAM CRAM

Christiane Nord, Translating as a Purposeful Activity, Ch. 1 | Essential Points Only

🎯 Core Argument

Central thesis: Functionalism did not appear overnight. It grew out of dissatisfaction with equivalence-based linguistic approaches, especially among scholars in translator training who found that professional translating includes many cases where equivalence is not called for at all. The chapter sketches the 'German School': Reiss, Vermeer's Skopostheorie, Holz-Mänttäri's translational action, and functionalist methodology.

📚 Key Terms & Concepts

Equivalence approaches: Source-text oriented; the ST's features (content, style, function) must be preserved in the TT (Catford, Nida & Taber, Wilss, Koller)
Koller's normative statement: Any target text that is not equivalent 'as far as possible' is declared a non-translation
Skopos: Greek for 'purpose'/'aim'; the technical term for the purpose of a translation. The prime principle determining any translation process is the purpose of the overall translational action
Status of the source text: Much lower than in equivalence theories. For Reiss the ST is 'the measure of all things'; for Vermeer it is an 'offer of information' partly or wholly turned into an offer of information for the target audience

🗝️ Rapid Q&A

Q: What dissatisfaction gave rise to functionalism?
A: Dissatisfaction with equivalence-based approaches among translator-training scholars, who found that professional practice includes many cases where equivalence is not wanted — e.g. a British school certificate translated for a German university is not expected to look or function like a German one.
Q: How did Reiss introduce a functional category, and what are her 'exceptions'?
A: Within an objective approach to translation criticism, she allowed exceptions from equivalence based on the translation brief: when the target text has a different function than the original, or addresses a different audience (e.g. Gulliver's Travels for children). She called these 'transfers'.
Q: Define Skopos and explain the status of the source text in Skopostheorie.
A: Skopos is the purpose of the translation — the prime principle determining the translation process. The source text's status is low: for Vermeer it is merely an 'offer of information' to be partly or wholly re-offered to the target audience.
Q: What is 'translational action' (Holz-Mänttäri)?
A: A complex, purpose-driven action in which translators, as experts, produce 'message transmitters' to coordinate communicative cooperation across language and culture barriers — emphasising participant roles and situational conditions.
Q: State Hönig & Kussmaul's key maxim.
A: The 'necessary degree of precision': reproduce just those semantic features that are relevant in the given context with regard to the function of your translation (in line with Grice's maxims).
🔥 Functionalism moves from equivalence to purpose. Reiss adds a functional category, Vermeer makes Skopos (purpose) the prime principle and demotes the ST to an 'offer of information', Holz-Mänttäri reframes it as translational action — all driven by translator training.
