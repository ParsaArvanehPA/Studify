⚡ Basic Aspects of Skopostheorie · EXAM CRAM

Christiane Nord, Translating as a Purposeful Activity, Ch. 3 | Essential Points Only

🎯 Core Argument

Central thesis: The prime principle of any translation is its Skopos (purpose). The chapter unpacks the core concepts of Vermeer's general theory (Skopos rule, coherence, culture) and Reiss's specific theories (adequacy vs. equivalence, text typology), showing how purpose governs the whole translation process.

📚 Key Terms & Concepts

Skopos: Greek for 'purpose'; the aim of the overall translational action. Usually refers to the purpose of the target text
Aim (Ziel): The final result an agent intends to achieve by an action
Purpose (Zweck): A provisional stage in attaining an aim (aim and purpose are relative)
Function (Funktion): What a text means / is intended to mean from the receiver's point of view
Intention (Absicht): An aim-oriented plan of action on the part of sender and receiver
Translation brief (Übersetzungsauftrag): The client's specification of the purpose — ideally detailing addressees, time, place, occasion, medium and intended function. Translation is normally done 'by assignment', with the client as initiator
Intratextual coherence (coherence rule): The target text must make sense in the communicative situation and culture in which it is received — 'coherent with' = 'part of' the receiver's situation
Intertextual coherence (fidelity rule): The relationship between the target text and the source text ('fidelity'); its form depends on the translator's interpretation of the ST and on the Skopos
Culture (Vermeer, after Göhring): Whatever one has to know, master or feel in order to behave according to a community's expectations (or to deviate from them). A complex system of norms and conventions
Cultureme: A social phenomenon of culture X, regarded as relevant by its members, that is found to be specific to X when compared with a corresponding phenomenon in culture Y (e.g. 'to have coffee' in England vs Spain vs Germany)
Adequacy (Reiss): A dynamic, process-oriented quality: the target text is 'adequate to' the requirements of the brief — the goal-oriented selection of signs appropriate for the communicative purpose. The superordinate concept
Equivalence: A static, result-oriented concept: a relationship of 'equal communicative value' between two texts. In Skopostheorie, equivalence = adequacy to a Skopos that requires the TT to serve the same function as the ST ('functional equivalence')

🗝️ Rapid Q&A

Q: State the Skopos rule and what it resolves.
A: A translational action is determined by its Skopos (purpose) — 'the end justifies the means'. It resolves the old dilemmas of free vs. faithful and dynamic vs. formal equivalence: the required approach depends entirely on the purpose of the translation.
Q: What is a translation brief, and what does it (not) specify?
A: The client's specification of the translation's purpose (addressees, time, place, medium, function). It specifies what is needed but not how to translate — strategy and type remain the translator's expert responsibility.
Q: Give the three rules of Skopostheorie and their hierarchy.
A: The Skopos rule (purpose governs), the coherence rule (intratextual coherence — the TT must make sense to its receivers), and the fidelity rule (intertextual coherence with the ST). Fidelity is subordinate to coherence, and both are subordinate to the Skopos rule.
Q: Distinguish adequacy from equivalence in Skopostheorie.
A: Adequacy is a dynamic, process quality — the TT being 'adequate to' the brief — and is the superordinate concept. Equivalence is a static, result-oriented relationship of equal value; here it is reduced to adequacy to a Skopos requiring the same function ('functional equivalence').
Q: Name Reiss's three text types and how each is translated.
A: Informative (inform — render content fully by TL norms), expressive (aesthetic — reproduce an analogous stylistic effect), and operative (persuade — aim for the same reaction, changing content/form if needed).
🔥 Purpose rules everything. Learn the hierarchy Skopos > intratextual coherence > fidelity, the translation brief (what not how), adequacy (to the brief) over equivalence, and Reiss's informative/expressive/operative text types.
